Why Upgrade Your Insulation?

The High Cost of Poor Building Insulation

Over 90% of residential buildings suffer from under-insulated attics and unsealed air penetrations. Proper thermal containment delivers immediate structural, financial, and acoustic advantages.

Permanent Bill Reduction

Stop paying for conditioned air that leaks through your ceiling. Proper R-values lower monthly heating and AC equipment runtime by 20% to 35%.

Even Room Temperatures

Eliminate hot second-floor rooms in summer and chilly, drafty floors in winter. Balance comfort consistently across every square foot of your living space.

Acoustic Soundproofing

Dense fiberglass and spray foam dampen exterior noise, including traffic rumble, airport flyovers, loud neighborhood activity, and heavy rain drumming.

Moisture & Mold Protection

Air leakage carries moist indoor air into cold attics and crawlspaces, generating condensation and wood rot. Precision air sealing blocks moisture migration.

Extended HVAC Lifespan

When your thermal envelope holds heat and cool air efficiently, your furnace and heat pump cycle less frequently, preventing premature mechanical failure.

Lower Carbon Emissions

Reducing building thermal load directly offsets thousands of pounds of grid power plant emissions, qualifying your property for federal eco rebates.

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, straightforward answers about building codes, R-values, and what to expect during installation.

If you can see your attic floor joists (the wooden framing beams), you almost certainly have less than 7–8 inches of insulation (R-19 or less). Modern building codes in most climate zones recommend between R-49 and R-60 (approximately 16 to 18 inches of dense-pack material). Other signs include ice dams on gutters, drafty rooms, and large temperature variations between floors.

Open-cell spray foam is lighter, less dense (0.5 lbs/cu ft), has an R-value of ~R-3.7 per inch, and is permeable to water vapor, making it great for interior sound dampening and roof decks in warm climates. Closed-cell spray foam is extremely dense (2.0 lbs/cu ft), provides ~R-6.5 to R-7.0 per inch, acts as a certified vapor and air barrier, and adds structural racking strength to walls.

Insulation materials like fiberglass and cellulose resist heat conduction, but they are not air barriers. Heated air naturally rises through chimneys, plumbing vents, ceiling cutouts, and recessed lights into the attic via the "stack effect." Air sealing with polyurethane foam blocks this airflow completely before insulation is installed on top.

For a standard 1,500 to 3,000 sq ft residential attic, prep, air sealing, baffle install, and blown-in fiberglass typically takes between 4 to 6 hours on a single day. If existing contaminated or rodent-damaged insulation requires full vacuum extraction, the project may require one additional day.
